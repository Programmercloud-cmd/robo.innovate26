4. Implement the residual policy interface

Deterministic kinematics performs the gross approach. The policy controls only bounded X/Y/Z translation and yaw relative to the nominal target. Keep the end effector vertical: no roll/pitch actions or silent relaxation of this constraint.

Use the existing absolute residual convention and smooth guarded interpolation.

Actor observations should include:
- brick index or derived goal features, nominal orientation and local support pattern;
- target-associated Logitech wrist-camera stud features in calibrated normalized image coordinates;
- feature confidence, visibility, correspondence and timestamps;
- measured target-relative TCP pose/velocity and relevant joint state;
- calibrated estimated wrench, gripper state and previous actions;
- a short history where needed for latency, occlusion and contact ambiguity.

Verify whether held-brick pose/slip is observable; lower-stud detections alone do not establish the brick-to-gripper transform.

Use the same observation meanings and processing on the robot and in simulation. If training from projected features, model visibility, detector errors, dropouts and latency using real camera evidence. Exact hidden brick poses, contact states and engagement labels may support training rewards or a privileged critic, but must not leak into actor observations.

Keep workspace, joint, singularity, force, tracking, stale-observation, grasp and robot-fault protections outside the policy. Recovery must be bounded and deterministic.

5. Train and evaluate

Once the task-specific model and observation/control interfaces pass their validation gates, actually run a bounded initial training job on the Brev VM.

Use a staged curriculum: aligned seating first, then measured handoff errors and the supporting patterns needed for the 12-brick alternating layout. Randomize measured uncertainty, not arbitrary ranges intended to hide an incorrect model.

Reward independently defensible seated engagement and retention. Penalize excessive loads, slip, jamming, unnecessary motion and time. Low TCP height, force drop or simulator attachment flags alone must not define success.

Report success, partial seating, jams, slips, recoverable stops, peak loads and duration. If vertical-only actions are insufficient, demonstrate that limitation rather than enabling tilt without authorization.
